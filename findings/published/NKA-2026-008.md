## Preconditions

- Hostile capsule holding the IPC bit
- Ability to spawn several threads (see NKA-2026-006)
- Halt variant only: machine RAM of about 256 MiB or less (hypothesis)

## Description

All inboxes together may hold 96 MiB, checked against nothing about free heap. A thread has its own inbox proc.<tid> that serves no endpoint, so MkIpcSendToPid from a sibling thread passes the gates (empty endpoint set passes inbox_admits). With about 12 (inbox, sender) pairs of 8 MiB shares, 96 messages of 1 MiB reach the total. The parked thread never reads, so the bytes stay. admit applies the total to the kernel's own sends too.

## Impact

System-wide IPC refusal (kernel-to-capsule traffic such as crypto, vfs, block returns BUSY) for as long as the threads live. On a machine with RAM <= 256 MiB the heap is 96 MiB, so the budget can take the whole heap and the OOM path powers off (hypothesis; not shown).

## Attack scenario

1. Capsule spawns about 12 threads that never read their inboxes.
2. One sender sends large messages to each sibling inbox until the per-(inbox, sender) share and then the 96 MiB system total are reached.
3. Large-message sends then fail system-wide with QueueFull; very small messages can still pass until the last few hundred KB of headroom are also used.

## Evidence

Reproduced: yes.

- A host model built against the real budget.rs reached 99.6 MB of 100.7 MB over 12 inboxes; confirmed by an independent code reading.
- Reproduced on a booted kernel: one process, using 12 sibling thread inboxes that are never drained, drove each inbox to its 8 MiB per-sender share (8 large sends each, 96 sends total, about 95.6 MiB), after which a further large send to a fresh inbox from the same or a different sender was refused (rc=-16) while the total held. One-byte sends were still accepted afterward, using the remaining headroom (roughly 0.4 MiB) before also failing; so large-message IPC is refused system-wide well before the stated 96 MiB is literally exhausted by every message size.
- The heap-exhaustion halt named in preconditions as a hypothesis was not reached in this test (8 GiB guest); it remains untested. Whether a killed capsule's bytes are freed by the reaper was not checked.

## Remediation

Tie the total to a fraction of free heap, add a per-process-group byte cap across inboxes, and refuse sends into inboxes whose owner is the sender's own group beyond a small bound.
